Introduction

For this project, I will use World Bank GDP data to compare the economic growth of the United States and China. The original dataset contains GDP figures organized by year in wide format, which I will transform into long format using tidyr. I will then analyze GDP trends over time to better understand how China’s economy has grown relative to the United States.

Documentation

I downloaded the GDP dataset from the World Bank’s World Development Indicators database https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/NY.GDP.MKTP.CD?locations=US-CN. The original dataset was in wide format with 2 rows and 70 columns, including yearly GDP data from 1960 to 2025.

I used read_excel() to import the data and skipped the first three rows to correctly identify the column names. I then removed unnecessary columns using select() and transformed the data from wide to long format using pivot_longer(). The final dataset contains 132 rows and 3 columns: Country Name, Year, and GDP.

I checked for missing values and found none. Finally, I used group_by() and summarise() to calculate average GDP for both countries and created a line graph with ggplot2 to compare their economic growth over time.

Transform Data from Wide to Long Format

# Transform yearly GDP columns into long format
gdp_long <- gdp_clean %>%
  pivot_longer(
    cols = `1960`:`2025`,
    names_to = "Year",
    values_to = "GDP"
  )

GDP Trends Over Time

ggplot(gdp_long, aes(
  x = as.numeric(Year),
  y = GDP / 1e12,
  color = `Country Name`
)) +
  geom_line() +
  labs(
    title = "U.S. vs. China GDP (1960–2025)",
    x = "Year",
    y = "GDP (Trillions of USD)",
    color = "Country"
  )

Interpretation of Trends

The United States has maintained a higher GDP than China throughout the period, but China’s economy has grown rapidly since the early 2000s. China’s GDP increased from around $1 trillion in 2000 to nearly $20 trillion in 2025, while the U.S. reached approximately $31 trillion. Although China has significantly narrowed the economic gap compared with earlier decades, the United States still maintains a substantial lead.

Conclusion

The analysis shows that although the United States has maintained a larger GDP, China has experienced significant economic growth over the past several decades. China’s rapid expansion demonstrates how much its economy has developed, even though it has not surpassed the United States in total GDP measured in current U.S. dollars.
